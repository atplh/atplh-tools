090 · COMMUNICATIONS

Comms reference

The things the exam asks most: transponder codes, distress and urgency, radio failure, light signals, read-backs and standard words. Based on ICAO Annex 10 and SERA.

Memory aid: 75 taken alive, 76 radio fix, 77 going to heaven. 1200 is the North American VFR code, not used in Europe.

Distress and urgency

Distress (MAYDAY ×3): grave and imminent danger, immediate assistance required.

Urgency (PAN PAN ×3): concerns safety, but no immediate assistance needed. PAN PAN MEDICAL for medical transports.

Distress message, in this order:

  1. Name of the station addressed (time permitting)
  2. Aircraft identification
  3. Nature of the emergency
  4. Intention of the person in command
  5. Present position, level and heading

First call on the frequency in use; otherwise 121.5 MHz. Squawk 7700.

Distress traffic control

STOP TRANSMITTING – MAYDAYImposes silence (by the aircraft in distress or the station in control)
MAYDAY RELAYPassing on a distress message for another station
DISTRESS TRAFFIC ENDEDCancels radio silence when the distress is over
CANCEL DISTRESSAircraft no longer in distress (also cancel urgency for PAN)

Radio failure

Always: squawk 7600, keep listening and transmit your position and intentions blind (“TRANSMITTING BLIND”) on the frequency in use.

VMC: continue in VMC, land at the nearest suitable aerodrome and report arrival to ATC by the quickest means.

IMC / IFR: keep the last assigned speed and level (or the minimum flight altitude, if higher) for:

  • 20 min after failing to report over a compulsory reporting point, where radar is not used
  • 7 min where radar is used, counted from the latest of: reaching the last assigned level, squawking 7600, or failing to report

Then continue in accordance with the flight plan. Check your syllabus for the full SERA.14083 procedure.

Mandatory read-back

  • ATC route clearances
  • Clearances to enter, land on, take off from, hold short of, cross, taxi or backtrack on any runway
  • Runway in use
  • Altimeter settings
  • SSR codes
  • Newly assigned frequencies
  • Level, heading and speed instructions
  • Transition levels

Wind, weather and traffic information do not need to be read back. End every read-back with your call sign.

Call signs

An aircraft may abbreviate its call sign only after the ground station has abbreviated it, once communication is established and no confusion is likely.

Registration call signs: first character plus at least the last two, e.g. G-ABCD → G-CD, N57826 → N26.

Airline call signs (telephony designator + flight number) are not abbreviated.
